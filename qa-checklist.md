# Final QA Checklist

Run before handing over the manuscript. Report to the user only the items that fail, with the fix; say one sentence if everything passes.

## Idea and title
- [ ] The idea takes the form of an idea/innovation, not research
- [ ] The title has no research-marker words
- [ ] The title is 25 words at most (target 20)
- [ ] The title contains the form/explanation of the idea, the problem, one goal (scope if needed)
- [ ] The title fits the theme and subtheme
- [ ] The acronym has a single meaning, is short, and has no expansion in parentheses

## Introduction
- [ ] 1.5-2 pages, ideally 5 paragraphs, 3-5 sentences per paragraph
- [ ] About 30% opinion and 70% data/facts
- [ ] Data and references from the last 5 years; the case matches the data year
- [ ] Contains the problem, urgency, novelty, strength of the idea, focus and direction of continuation
- [ ] Connects directly to the discussion
- [ ] Has the key functions: argument + data, shortcomings of the old solution, the opening, one potential solution, a transition sentence that states the whole idea
- [ ] The opening quotation (if any) is real and verifiable

## Discussion
- [ ] Explains the idea, implementation, advantages, and difference from existing ideas
- [ ] Has supporting analysis (SWOT, competitors, SMART) as needed
- [ ] Has a prototype/design/features/content fitting the type of idea
- [ ] Explains fulfillment of the theme and subtheme
- [ ] Has a success projection and a statement of innovation and creativity
- [ ] Length matches the allocation; the excess is moved to the appendix
- [ ] Not in the style of a research report
- [ ] How it works can be pictured: sequence of steps, tools/materials, and who carries it out
- [ ] The comparison table uses the same aspects on both sides, is sourced, and is not one-sided
- [ ] Feasibility contains assumptions and sources (materials, cost, policy) and honestly acknowledged challenges
- [ ] Impact/SDGs (if used) are specific contributions, 3-4 at most, in the body only, not in the title
- [ ] The projection is phased: time frame, target, form of development
- [ ] Benefit figures are written as other people's findings or as projections, not as the author's test results

## Compliance
- [ ] Meets the page/word limit, format, and section order of the competition guidelines
- [ ] All claims and figures are sourced and cited; no invented data; any remaining `[DATA NEEDED]` is reported to the user

## Figures, tables, and common thread
- [ ] There is one main figure that answers "how does this work", fitting the type of idea (visuals.md); no more than two supporting figures
- [ ] Figures and tables are numbered in sequence without jumps, cited in the text, and carry a caption and source
- [ ] Figure labels are legible when printed (font at least 8 pt) and use the same terms as the text
- [ ] Figures made with AI tools are checked for spelling and fit, and comply with the guidelines
- [ ] The title keywords (idea, problem, goal) reappear in introduction P5, B1, B8, and the closing
- [ ] The challenges in feasibility are answered by a step in how it works or a stage in the projection
- [ ] Technical terms use one name throughout the manuscript

## Writing style
- [ ] No banned words or phrases in the body (style-guide.md); the title is exempt per the mentor pattern
- [ ] Sentence length varies; no three consecutive sentences of equal length or three short sentences in a row
- [ ] No "not just X, but Y" pattern and no forced grouping of three
- [ ] At most one em dash per 500 words
- [ ] No invented anecdotes, quotations, or figures
