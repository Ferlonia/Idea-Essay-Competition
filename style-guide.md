# Manuscript Writing Style: Condensed Anti-AI-Pattern Version

Contents: position of this file, structure rules, punctuation, vocabulary and phrases, adjustments for scientific essays, final check.

## Position of this file
This is a summary sufficient to keep the manuscript clean. If the full anti-AI-writing skill and its complete banned-word list are installed in the same session, follow the full one; this file is a backup and does not replace it. The aim is to make the writing read as the author's own work: specific, varied, and honest. No method guarantees passing an AI detector, and some competitions prohibit AI use or require disclosing it; follow the guidelines.

## Structure rules
1. Vary sentence length. Do not write three consecutive sentences of the same length; mix sentences of 5-8 words with sentences of 25-30 words.
2. Avoid a run of short sentences that each stand alone. Join related thoughts with conjunctions, subordinate clauses, semicolons, or commas, so that cause, contrast, and condition are visible.
3. Do not group things in threes by reflex. State the number that really exists: two, four, one.
4. Take a position; do not weigh two sides equally. State the counterargument in one sentence, then continue.
5. Vary paragraph shapes: some start with a rhetorical question, some with data, some are a single sentence. Not every paragraph needs a transition sentence; some may stop at their content.
6. Use active sentences with a clear actor where possible ("the farmer group collects", not "is collected"). Passive stays acceptable for procedures and findings where the actor does not matter.
7. Bullet lists only when the content really is a list; in an essay manuscript, prefer paragraphs.
8. The tone of someone who works with data, not a brochure. No cheerleading.

## Punctuation
- Em dash (—): at most one per 500 words; otherwise use commas, semicolons, colons, or a new sentence.
- Exclamation marks: not used in a scientific manuscript.
- Ellipsis: not used except for a truncated quotation.
- Semicolons and colons: allowed and encouraged; use a colon to set up content that is stated immediately after.

## Specific, not general
- Use real figures, names, places, years, and sources. "BPS (2024) recorded ..." is better than "data shows".
- Write concrete workings: who does what, where, in how many steps.
- Do not invent personal stories, quotes, or data. If a hypothetical example is needed, say so ("suppose a farmer group has 20 members") and mark it as an illustration only.
- Choose the less predictable word when there are equal options; do not always take the first word that comes up.

## Vocabulary and phrases to avoid in the body of the manuscript

### For Indonesian manuscripts
Words: mendalami/menyelami (delve), tapestri, lanskap (figurative), krusial (crucial), rumit (figurative), memperkuat (bolster), menegaskan (underscore), multidimensi, memupuk (foster), memanfaatkan as a filler verb (just "memakai" or "menggunakan" if needed), memfasilitasi, mencakup, sangat penting, terobosan (groundbreaking), mutakhir (cutting-edge), mengubah permainan (game-changer), transformatif, merevolusi, mulus (seamless), tangguh (outside engineering), komprehensif (to praise one's own work), luar biasa, menakjubkan, mendalam (figurative), sinergi, titik masalah (pain points), ke depannya (moving forward).
Phrases: "perlu dicatat bahwa", "penting untuk dicatat", "pada intinya", "dalam ranah", "ketika berbicara soal", "bukti nyata dari", "bukan hanya X, tetapi Y", "bukan sekadar X, tetapi Y", "di sinilah X berperan", "pada akhirnya", "intinya adalah", "menjembatani kesenjangan", "membuka potensi", "memberdayakan", "kesimpulannya", "secara keseluruhan" (as a paragraph opener), a run of "pertama... kedua... ketiga...".
Paragraph openers to avoid: "Selain itu,", "Lebih lanjut,", "Tambahan lagi,", "Menariknya,", "Perlu dicatat,", "Yang penting,", "Namun, penting untuk ...".
Common sentence openers that repeat in machine writing: "Dalam", "Dari", "Berdasarkan", "Ini". Do not ban them outright (a scientific manuscript naturally uses "Berdasarkan ... (BPS, 2024)"), but do not open two consecutive paragraphs with the same word.

### For English manuscripts [ADDED]
Words: delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), crucial, intricate, bolster, underscore, multifaceted, foster, leverage, utilize (just "use"), facilitate, encompass, paramount, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, revolutionize, seamless, robust (outside engineering), comprehensive (to praise one's own work), remarkable, stunning, profound, synergy, pain points, moving forward.
Phrases: "it is worth noting that", "it is important to note", "in essence", "in the realm of", "when it comes to", "a testament to", "not only X but also Y", "not just X, but Y", "this is where X comes in", "at the end of the day", "the bottom line is", "bridging the gap", "unlock the potential", "empower", "in conclusion", "overall," (as a paragraph opener), a run of "firstly... secondly... thirdly...".
Paragraph openers to avoid: "Additionally,", "Furthermore,", "Moreover,", "Interestingly,", "Notably,", "However, it is important to ...".

## Adjustments for scientific essays (competition rules beat a casual style)
- The title follows the mentor pattern and is exempt from the vocabulary list above: words such as "Pemanfaatan" (Utilization), "Peningkatan" (Improvement), "Memaksimalkan" (Maximizing), and "Mengurangi" (Reducing) are title patterns judged good and stay in the title. In the body, use more concrete words.
- Formal register (bahasa baku in Indonesian): no colloquial abbreviations; "tidak", not "nggak". The "use contractions" rule of casual style does not apply; in English drafts, also avoid contractions.
- Write contrast as two ordinary sentences, not the "not X, but Y" pattern. For example: "The old way only works after the problem appears. This idea works on its cause."
- A summarizing sentence at the end of a section is allowed, but vary its opener (not always "In other words" or "Thus").
- An opening quotation only if it is real and verifiable.
- No excessive sub-headings: follow the competition's section order (PENDAHULUAN, PEMBAHASAN, PENUTUP: Introduction, Discussion, Closing) and add subheadings only as needed.

## Final check before handing over the text
1. Any banned word or phrase above? Replace it with something concrete.
2. Three consecutive sentences of similar length? Change them.
3. Three or more short sentences in a row? Join them.
4. A forced grouping of three? Break it.
5. More than one em dash per 500 words? Reduce.
6. Any invented figure, source, or story? Remove or mark it.
7. Could anyone have written it for any topic? Add details that fit only this idea.
